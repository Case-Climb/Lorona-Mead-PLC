If you were arrested for DUI in Scottsdale over Halloween weekend, the most important thing to know right now is this: you likely have **only 15 days from the date you were served with the license suspension notice to request a hearing with the Arizona Motor Vehicle Division (MVD)**. If you miss that window, your driving privileges can be suspended automatically, no matter what later happens in court. Talking with a Scottsdale DUI lawyer early can help you protect that deadline and understand your options.

The second thing to know is that an arrest is not a conviction. A DUI case in Arizona has two separate tracks: a criminal case in court and an administrative case with MVD. Each has its own rules, deadlines, and possible outcomes. Many people feel overwhelmed in the first few days, but the steps that matter most are manageable once you know what they are.

This guide walks through why Halloween weekend arrests are common in Scottsdale, how the two tracks work, and a calm checklist of what to do this week.

## Why DUI Arrests Spike Over Halloween Weekend in Scottsdale

Halloween is one of the busier party weekends of the year in the Valley, and Scottsdale draws large crowds to Old Town bars, restaurants, and costume events. Law enforcement agencies in Arizona commonly increase DUI patrols and task-force operations around major holidays and event weekends, and Halloween is often one of them. In 2026, Halloween falls on a Saturday, which means the celebrations stretch across Friday and Saturday nights.

More officers on the road means more traffic stops, and more stops mean more DUI investigations. That includes people who believed they were fine to drive, people stopped for minor issues like a burned-out taillight, and drivers who were tested for drugs or medications rather than alcohol.

Increased enforcement does not change the legal standards. The state still has to prove its case, and officers still have to follow the rules for stops, investigations, and testing. Busy enforcement nights can sometimes mean rushed paperwork or procedural mistakes, which is one reason every case deserves a careful review.

## The Two Tracks of an Arizona DUI Case

### The criminal case

The criminal case is the prosecution for the DUI charge itself, such as impaired driving or driving with a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or more under A.R.S. § 28-1381. Higher BAC levels can lead to "extreme" DUI charges under A.R.S. § 28-1382, and certain circumstances can make a DUI a felony ("aggravated DUI") under A.R.S. § 28-1383.

Most misdemeanor DUI arrests made by Scottsdale police are handled in Scottsdale City Court, though the court depends on where the arrest happened and which agency made it. Your paperwork should list your court and first appearance date (often called an arraignment). For a deeper look at what follows a first arrest, see our guide on [what happens after a first offense DUI in Arizona](/blog/first-offense-dui-arizona-what-to-expect).

### The MVD administrative case

Separately, MVD can suspend your license based on the arrest itself. If you took a test showing a BAC of 0.08% or more, this is usually called an "admin per se" suspension. If you refused testing, it falls under Arizona's implied consent law, and the suspension period is generally longer.

This administrative suspension is not a criminal penalty, and it moves on its own timeline. That is where the 15-day deadline comes in.

## The 15-Day Deadline to Request an MVD Hearing

When you are arrested for DUI in Arizona, officers typically give you a pink form, often called the Admin Per Se/Implied Consent Affidavit. Read it carefully. In most cases it does three things:

1. Notifies you that MVD intends to suspend your driving privileges.
2. Serves as a temporary driving permit for a short period, usually 15 days.
3. Explains that you can request a hearing to contest the suspension.

Under Arizona law, the request for a hearing generally must be received by MVD within 15 days of the date you were served with the notice. If you request it in time, the suspension is usually put on hold until the hearing is decided. If you do not, the suspension typically takes effect automatically once the temporary permit runs out.

A few practical points:

- **Count from the date on the notice**, not the date you were released or the date you found the paperwork in your car.
- **Weekends and holidays still count.** With a Halloween weekend arrest, the clock is already running.
- **Keep proof** that your request was submitted and received.
- **Rules and forms can change**, so confirm the current process with MVD or a lawyer rather than relying on memory or an old website.

The MVD hearing is also valuable beyond your license. It can be an early opportunity to hear the officer's account under oath, which may help in the criminal case. Whether requesting a hearing makes sense in your situation is a good question to raise during a consultation.

## Your Calm Next-Steps Checklist

Here is a simple list to work through in the days after your arrest. You do not have to do everything in one afternoon, but don't put it off either.

1. **Gather your paperwork.** Find the citation, the pink MVD notice, any release documents, and anything about your vehicle if it was impounded. Take photos of each page.
2. **Mark your deadlines.** Write down the 15-day MVD hearing deadline and your court date. Put them in your phone with reminders.
3. **Write down what you remember.** While it is fresh, note where you were, what and when you ate and drank, when you were stopped, what the officer said, what tests you were asked to do, and whether you took a breath or blood test. Keep these notes private and share them only with your attorney.
4. **Do not discuss the case publicly.** Avoid posting about the arrest, your Halloween night, or your costume party on social media. Avoid talking about details with anyone other than your lawyer.
5. **Keep receipts and records.** Bar or restaurant receipts, rideshare history, and texts showing timing can sometimes matter. Save them, but do not delete anything.
6. **Do not drive on a suspended license.** Once a suspension takes effect, driving can lead to new charges that are often more serious than the original DUI.
7. **Talk to a criminal defense lawyer.** An attorney can explain the specific charges, review the stop and testing for problems, and help with both the MVD and court tracks.
8. **Attend every court date.** Missing court can lead to a warrant. If you hire a lawyer, they can tell you whether your personal appearance is required.

If your arrest also involved other allegations, such as a weapon in the vehicle or suspected drugs, mention that right away, since it can affect how the case is handled.

## What a Scottsdale DUI Lawyer Can Look At

Every case is different, and no lawyer can promise a particular result. But an experienced defense attorney will usually examine questions like these:

- **Was the traffic stop lawful?** Officers generally need reasonable suspicion to pull you over.
- **Were field sobriety tests given and scored properly?** Footwear, costumes, weather, medical conditions, and nerves can all affect performance.
- **Was the breath or blood test handled correctly?** Testing devices must be maintained and used according to procedures, and blood samples must be collected, stored, and analyzed properly.
- **Were your rights respected?** This includes issues around your right to counsel and how any refusal was handled.
- **What does the evidence actually show?** Body camera and dash camera video, reports, and lab results sometimes tell a different story than the citation.

Depending on what the review finds, options may include challenging evidence, negotiating with the prosecutor, or taking the case to trial. Penalties for a DUI conviction in Arizona can include jail time, fines, an ignition interlock device, alcohol screening and classes, and license consequences.
