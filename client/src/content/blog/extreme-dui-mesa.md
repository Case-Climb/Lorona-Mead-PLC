If you were arrested in Mesa and your breath or blood test came back at 0.15 or higher, you are likely facing an **extreme DUI** charge instead of a standard DUI. In Arizona, an extreme DUI Mesa case carries mandatory jail time, a longer ignition interlock requirement, and higher fines than a regular first offense. If the result was 0.20 or higher, the charge is often called a "super extreme" DUI, and the minimum penalties increase again.

That sounds alarming, and it is serious. But a charge is not a conviction. A BAC number is a piece of evidence, not an automatic verdict, and there are well-established ways to test whether that number is reliable. Many extreme DUI cases turn on questions about timing, testing procedures, and the lawfulness of the stop.

This article explains how Arizona defines extreme and super extreme DUI, what penalties are typically at stake, what happens to your driver's license, and how BAC evidence can be challenged. It is general information, not legal advice for your specific case.

## What Counts as an Extreme DUI in Arizona?

Arizona's extreme DUI law is found at A.R.S. § 28-1382. In general terms, it makes it a crime to drive or be in actual physical control of a vehicle with an alcohol concentration of:

- **0.15 or more but less than 0.20** within two hours of driving (commonly called "extreme DUI"), or
- **0.20 or more** within two hours of driving (commonly called "super extreme DUI").

Two details matter here:

1. **The two-hour window.** The State generally must show that your BAC was at or above the threshold within two hours of driving. Delays between the stop and the test can become an important issue.
2. **"Actual physical control."** You do not always have to be driving down the road. People have been charged while sitting in a parked car with the engine running. Whether someone was truly in "actual physical control" depends on all the circumstances.

A first or second extreme DUI is usually a class 1 misdemeanor, the most serious misdemeanor level in Arizona. Certain factors, such as a suspended license, a child under 15 in the vehicle, or prior DUI convictions within a set period, can turn a DUI into an aggravated DUI, which is a felony. If you are unsure which category you may be facing, our guide on [felony vs. misdemeanor charges in Arizona](/blog/felony-vs-misdemeanor-arizona) explains the difference.

## Extreme vs. Super Extreme DUI: How the Penalties Compare

Arizona sets mandatory minimum penalties for DUI offenses, and they rise with the BAC level. The figures below describe how first offenses typically work, but sentencing laws, fees, and assessments can change, and individual courts have some discretion. Treat these as a general overview and get advice about your own situation. For a broader look at all DUI levels, see [Understanding Arizona's DUI Penalties](/blog/understanding-arizona-dui-penalties).

### First-offense extreme DUI (0.15 to 0.199)

- **Jail:** A minimum of 30 consecutive days. However, the judge may suspend all but 9 days if the person installs a certified ignition interlock device for 12 months.
- **Ignition interlock:** Typically required for 12 months.
- **Fines and assessments:** Generally totaling several thousand dollars once state assessments and fees are added.
- **Other requirements:** Alcohol screening, education or treatment, and possible community restitution.

### First-offense super extreme DUI (0.20 or higher)

- **Jail:** A minimum of 45 consecutive days, with the possibility of the judge suspending all but 14 days if an ignition interlock device is installed.
- **Ignition interlock:** Typically required for 18 months.
- **Fines and assessments:** Higher than for a 0.15 offense, again commonly totaling several thousand dollars.
- **Other requirements:** Alcohol screening and education or treatment are typical.

### Second offenses

A second extreme or super extreme DUI within the statutory look-back period carries much longer mandatory jail terms, often measured in months rather than days, along with higher fines and longer license and interlock consequences. Prior DUI history is one of the biggest factors in how a case is charged and resolved.

Some courts allow portions of a jail sentence to be served through home detention with alcohol monitoring after an initial period in jail, or through work release. Whether that is available depends on the court and your circumstances, so it is worth asking about early.

## What Happens to Your Driver's License?

An extreme DUI arrest usually starts two separate tracks: the criminal case in court and an administrative case with the Arizona Motor Vehicle Division (MVD).

### The administrative suspension

If you took a breath or blood test and the result was 0.08 or higher, the officer typically serves you with a notice of suspension. For a first test failure, this is commonly a 90-day suspension, with a portion that may be converted to a restricted license after the initial period if you complete alcohol screening. If you refused testing under Arizona's implied consent law, the suspension is generally much longer, often 12 months.

### The deadline to request a hearing

You have the right to request an MVD hearing to challenge the suspension, but the deadline is short and is printed on the paperwork you were given. If you miss it, the suspension usually takes effect automatically. Checking that deadline is one of the first things to do after an arrest.

### Ignition interlock and reinstatement

To get full driving privileges back after an extreme DUI conviction, you will generally need to install and maintain an ignition interlock device for the required period, carry proof of insurance (often an SR-22 filing), and complete any required screening or classes. The interlock period can be extended if the device records violations, so following the device rules closely matters.

## How BAC Results Can Be Challenged

Because the charge depends so heavily on a number, the reliability of that number deserves close attention. These are some of the issues a defense attorney commonly examines in an extreme DUI case. Not every issue applies to every case, and none guarantees a particular outcome.

### The legality of the stop and arrest

Police need reasonable suspicion to stop a vehicle and probable cause to make a DUI arrest. If the stop or arrest was not lawful, evidence gathered afterward, including the test result, may be challenged through a motion to suppress.

### Breath test procedures

Arizona breath testing follows specific procedures. Questions that may be relevant include:

- Was there a proper observation (deprivation) period before the test, so that burping, vomiting, or mouth alcohol did not skew the result?
- Did the two breath samples agree closely enough with each other?
- Was the machine properly calibrated and maintained, and do the maintenance records support its accuracy?
- Was the operator properly certified?

### Blood test procedures

Blood tests are often viewed as more precise, but they can still go wrong. Issues can include how and by whom the blood was drawn, whether the sample was properly preserved and labeled, the chain of custody, and the lab's analysis methods. In many cases, a portion of the sample can be independently retested.

### Timing and rising BAC

Alcohol continues to be absorbed for a period after drinking stops. Someone whose BAC was below 0.15 while driving may test higher later. When the test happened near or outside the two-hour window, or when drinking ended shortly before driving, the timeline can become a central issue. Expert analysis is sometimes used to evaluate this.

### Medical and physical factors

Conditions such as acid reflux, certain diets, or dental work can sometimes affect breath test results. These factors are fact-specific and need careful review.

### Negotiated outcomes

Even when the evidence is strong, weaknesses in part of the State's case can sometimes lead to a resolution on a less serious charge, such as a standard DUI instead of an extreme DUI, which can mean less mandatory jail and a shorter interlock period. Whether that is possible depends entirely on the facts and the evidence.

## How Extreme DUI Cases Move Through Mesa Courts

Where your case is heard depends on where the arrest happened and how it was charged. Misdemeanor DUIs arising within Mesa city limits are typically handled in Mesa Municipal Court. Arrests elsewhere in the area may go to a Maricopa County justice court, and felony aggravated DUI cases go to Maricopa County Superior Court.

A typical misdemeanor case involves an arraignment, a period of discovery when the defense reviews police reports, video, and testing records, pretrial conferences, possible motions, and then either a negotiated resolution or a trial. If this is your first time through the system, [what happens after a first-offense DUI arrest](/blog/first-offense-dui-arizona-what-to-expect) walks through the early stages in more detail.

## Practical Steps to Take Now

While your case is pending, a few things can help protect your options:

1. **Find your paperwork.** Locate the citation, release papers, and any MVD suspension notice. Note your court date and the hearing request deadline.
2. **Write down what you remember.** Record when you started and stopped drinking, what you ate, when you were stopped, and when testing happened. Timing details fade quickly.
3. **Do not discuss the case online.** Social media posts and messages can become evidence.
4. **Be cautious about early alcohol screening and interlock decisions.** These can affect both your license and your sentence, so it helps to get advice about the timing.
5. **Talk to a defense attorney before your first court date.** Early review can preserve evidence such as video footage and testing records.
